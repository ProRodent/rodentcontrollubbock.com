Same-Day Rodent Removal in Lubbock: How It Works

By Lubbock Rodent Control · Updated November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Same-day rodent removal in Lubbock is one of the most common questions we get: how fast, what's involved, and what happens after the technician leaves. This guide answers all three.

How same-day dispatch works.

Methodical wins.

Call (806) 207-3665. We triage on the phone: live rat in the living space, attic activity after a cold front, or dead-rodent odor, and set the dispatch priority accordingly. For daytime calls received before 2 PM, we dispatch within 2-4 hours for most Lubbock County addresses. Calls received later in the afternoon are typically scheduled for early the next morning unless the situation is a genuine emergency (live rat in the living space, active colony in a commercial kitchen facing a health inspection). After-hours emergency dispatch is available any hour for living-space situations; attic-only activity may be scheduled for the next morning.

What happens when the technician arrives.

The same-day visit is a full inspection and initial treatment, with the quote on top. The technician does a complete property walk, attic, exterior perimeter, interior areas of reported activity, identifies the species from physical evidence, maps the entry points, and deploys initial treatment (bait stations or snap traps on confirmed runways) during the same visit. The primary entry point is closed with appropriate materials before the technician leaves. This is more thorough than a "look and quote" visit.

Need a free inspection? Call (806) 207-3665, same-day service available across Lubbock County and 24 nearby South Plains towns.

What happens after the same-day visit.

The same-day visit handles the immediate situation. The follow-up program completes the solution: a return at 10-14 days to verify the colony is eliminated, close any secondary entry points, and complete the exclusion sealing that prevents recurrence. The follow-up is scheduled during the same-day visit before the technician leaves. For established colonies, a third visit at 14-21 days may be needed.

Geographic coverage.

Same-day service is available for all 31 Lubbock neighborhoods and for most of the nearby South Plains towns within 30 minutes: Wolfforth, Shallowater, Idalou, New Deal, Slaton, Ransom Canyon. Towns farther out (Levelland, Abernathy, Post) typically receive same-day service for daytime calls but may be scheduled for the following morning for late-afternoon calls.

To schedule: Call (806) 207-3665. Same-day availability is confirmed on the call. Inspections are always free. No surprise invoices, the quote is confirmed before work starts.

What a same-day visit should cover, per public guidance

The CDC's seal-up, trap-up, clean-up sequence is a good checklist for any rodent visit. Seal holes (mice fit through a hole the size of a nickel, rats through about half a quarter), use snap traps where activity is found, and clean up droppings safely rather than sweeping or vacuuming them.

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension adds that trapping needs near-daily maintenance, which is why a same-day visit is usually the start of a program and not the end of one. Ask what follow-up visits are scheduled and what will be sealed.

What the technician brings on a same-day call.

A same-day dispatch is a complete service: the technician arrives with a full equipment load. For rat calls, that means bait stations, snap traps sized for Norway rats and roof rats, hardware cloth and copper mesh for immediate exclusion of confirmed entry points, a flashlight and inspection mirror for attic and crawl-space assessment, and a camera for documentation. For mouse calls, the load includes snap traps in multiple sizes, copper mesh for weep-hole sealing on-site, and the inspection tools needed to map entry points before placing a single trap.

The inspection-first sequence is non-negotiable on same-day calls. Placing traps before mapping the colony produces incomplete results, you catch the animals nearest the trap, but you don't address the colony's main runway or the entry point that's still bringing new animals in. A same-day call that includes a 20-minute inspection before any treatment is placed produces better 14-day outcomes than one that skips straight to trap deployment.

Same-day service vs. scheduled service, when each makes sense.

Same-day service is the right choice when the situation creates immediate human risk or significant property damage risk: live rodents in living spaces, evidence of wire chewing near active electrical panels, or an active colony in a commercial kitchen facing a health inspection. It's also appropriate when the colony has progressed to the point that a population is establishing: multi-room droppings, multiple-day audible activity, or signs of nesting in living-space insulation or furniture.

Scheduled service is appropriate when evidence is contained: droppings in one location, single-room activity, or suspected entry point activity without confirmed interior presence. Scheduled appointments allow for more full pre-inspection documentation, better technician preparation for complex properties, and more flexibility on timing for properties that require access coordination. For property managers and commercial operators, scheduled programs with monitoring visits are almost always preferable to reactive same-day calls, because they catch activity before it becomes an emergency.

What drives the quote for same-day service.

Pricing reflects property reality.

Same-day rodent service in Lubbock is priced on the same structure as scheduled service, the treatment cost is the same, and the inspection is free. The difference is prioritization: same-day calls receive dispatch ahead of scheduled appointments and may incur an after-hours dispatch fee for calls placed after 5PM requesting immediate response. Daytime same-day calls (before 2PM) carry no scheduling premium. The treatment quote is confirmed after inspection and before any work begins, a same-day situation doesn't override the policy of quoting before starting, because agreeing to an unknown cost in an emergency situation is how homeowners end up with inflated invoices. What varies between same-day and scheduled programs is the follow-up structure, not the per-treatment cost: same-day calls typically have a next-day or 48-hour follow-up scheduled at booking rather than the 10-14 day follow-up for standard programs, because the same-day dispatch is designed to address immediate risk while the full program follows the standard timeline.
