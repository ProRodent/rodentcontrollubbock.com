Lubbock rodent control October checklist: before the first cold front hits.

By Lubbock Rodent Control · Published November 17, 2026 · 7 min read
Free inspection Written quote before work Locally Owned Open 24/7

Key takeaways

  • October is the highest-value month for prevention: Roof rats enter attics soon after the first major cold front, and exclusion done in October stops the problem before it starts.
  • Four priority areas: Soffit vents and fascia gaps, weep holes in brick construction, attic access points, and door sweeps and thresholds.
  • Weep holes are the most commonly missed entry point: Every brick-veneer home in Lubbock has them. They're 3/4 inch wide, the exact size a house mouse needs.
  • Self-inspection takes 45 minutes: Exterior perimeter walk, roof-line visual, attic hatch check, and door sweep inspection. We've listed every checkpoint below.
  • Professional inspection is free: If you find evidence or aren't sure what you're looking for, call us before the cold front arrives, not after.

Lubbock's rodent season doesn't start gradually, it starts with a cold front. When temperatures drop sharply in October, roof rats that have been living in tree canopy and exterior vegetation move toward attic access points within 48 hours. House mice, which have been entering homes sporadically all year, experience a significant entry-point surge as cold weather makes interior spaces attractive. The week after each major cold front is reliably the highest call-volume week of the year.

The good news: the entry points are predictable, the inspection is straightforward, and October is the correct time to address them, before the cold front, not after. This checklist covers every inspection point in order of priority.

Priority 1: Soffit vents and fascia gaps.

Roof rats enter attics almost exclusively from above-grade access points: degraded soffit vents, gaps at the fascia-soffit joint, and worn gable vents are the three most common. Walk the perimeter of your home and look up at the roof line from ground level. Soffit vent screens on homes built before 1990 are typically fiberglass or aluminum that degrades over decades of Lubbock's UV exposure and haboob impact. Look for screen tears, missing screen sections, or vent frames pulled away from the soffit. Fascia gaps, where the fascia board meets the soffit panel, are often invisible from ground level but visible from a ladder at eave height. Any opening larger than 1/2 inch at roof line requires attention before October cold fronts.

Hardware cloth (1/4-inch galvanized) is the correct sealing material for soffit vents and fascia gaps. Foam sealant is inadequate, roof rats will chew through it. Sheet metal flashing is appropriate for larger fascia separations. This is the highest-priority item on the checklist because roof-rat attic entry through these points is the a common and damaging infestation pattern in Lubbock.

Priority 2: Weep holes in brick-veneer construction.

Every brick-veneer home in Lubbock has weep holes at the foundation course, small drainage gaps in the mortar line at the base of the brick wall that open directly into the wall cavity. They are standard construction. They are 3/4 inch wide. A house mouse requires 1/4 inch. Weep holes are the single most common house-mouse entry point in Lubbock residential construction, and they're present on every brick home regardless of age or condition.

Walk the perimeter of your home at the foundation level and look for the gap pattern in the mortar line, typically every 4 to 6 bricks along the bottom course. Count the number of weep holes on each wall face. The correct sealing material is copper mesh pressed into the opening. It allows the drainage function the weep hole provides while blocking rodent entry. Hardware cloth cut to size works for larger openings. Do not use foam or caulk, which seal the drainage function and degrade quickly. This inspection takes 10 minutes and is the single highest-impact DIY exclusion task available to a Lubbock homeowner.

Priority 3: Attic access points and gable vents.

In addition to the eave-line access points, check the attic hatch or pull-down stair. Most attic hatches have a simple door with no gasket, roof rats can and do enter through the gap around an unsecured hatch. Weatherstripping around the hatch frame, combined with a positive-close latch, eliminates this access point. Gable vents, the triangular vents at the peak of the end walls, should be inspected for screen condition. Degraded or missing gable vent screens are less common than degraded soffit screens but are significant entry points when present, because gable vents open directly into the main attic volume at height where roof rats operate.

Priority 4: Door sweeps and thresholds.

House mice enter at ground level through gaps at door bottoms that most homeowners don't register as significant. A 1/4-inch gap under a door, common with standard door sweeps that have worn down over years of use, is a reliable house-mouse entry point. Check the door sweeps on all exterior doors, including the garage-to-house door, which is often overlooked. A worn door sweep is visible: hold the door closed and look for daylight underneath. Replacement door sweeps are inexpensive and install in under 10 minutes. Check garage door bottom seals as well, the rubber seal at the base of the garage door compresses over time and can develop gaps at the ends or center that provide house-mouse access into the garage.

What to do if you find evidence during the inspection.

If your October inspection turns up active evidence: droppings, chew marks on soffit material, or roof-line access points with fresh gnaw marks, treat it as an active colony requiring treatment before exclusion, so exclusion alone is not enough. Sealing entry points with an active interior population traps animals inside and creates a dead-rodent odor problem within days. Frequently asked questions.

When exactly should I do this inspection in Lubbock?
First two weeks of October. The first major cold front in Lubbock typically arrives in mid-to-late October, and roof-rat attic entry events begin within 48 hours of that first significant temperature drop. An inspection completed in the first two weeks of October catches and addresses entry points before the pressure event occurs.
How do I tell if my soffit vents need replacing vs. just cleaning?
If the screen material is brittle, discolored, has visible tears, or can be pushed inward with finger pressure, it needs replacing. Clogged but intact screen is a ventilation issue, not an exclusion issue. The test is structural: can the screen resist the sustained pressure a rodent would apply? If not, replace it with hardware cloth.
Can I seal weep holes permanently without causing moisture problems?
Copper mesh allows airflow and drainage while blocking rodent entry, it does not permanently seal the weep hole. It is the correct material precisely because it maintains the weep hole's drainage function while closing the rodent-entry dimension. Do not use caulk or foam, which permanently seal the drainage function and can cause moisture accumulation in the wall cavity.
What's the difference between roof-rat prevention and mouse prevention?
Roof rats enter from above: soffit vents, fascia gaps, gable vents, and any above-grade opening. House mice enter from the foundation level: weep holes, utility penetrations, door sweeps, and foundation gaps. Both are addressed by this checklist. Roof-rat prevention focuses on the roof line; mouse prevention focuses on the foundation perimeter and door thresholds.
What if I find active evidence during the inspection?
Call us before sealing. Sealing an active entry point with animals inside traps them in the wall or attic and creates a dead-rodent odor problem within 48-72 hours. We confirm interior population status, treat if needed, and then seal entry points in the correct sequence. The inspection is free.
Is this inspection something I can do myself?
Yes, for the ground-level and ladder-accessible inspection points. Soffit and fascia inspection from a standard ladder is straightforward. The weep-hole inspection and door-sweep check require no tools. The attic hatch and gable vent inspection are DIY-manageable. If you find worn soffit vents or want to seal weep holes correctly, we can walk you through the materials needed.

Why October is the time for sealing work

The CDC's seal-up guidance is simple: mice can fit through a hole the size of a nickel, and rats through a hole about half the size of a quarter. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension adds that house mice do not hibernate and nest in warm areas close to food, so they look for shelter as nights cool.

An October walk-around with those two measurements in mind catches most of the openings that matter: roof vents, door sweeps, utility lines, and foundation gaps. Sealing before the first hard cold front is easier than sealing around a colony that has already moved in.
