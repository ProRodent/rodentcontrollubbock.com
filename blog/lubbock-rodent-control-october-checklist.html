Lubbock rodent control October checklist: before the first cold front hits.

Written by Lubbock Rodent Control. Updated November 2026.
By Lubbock Rodent Control · Published November 17, 2026 · 7 min read
Open 24/7 Same-day service Locally owned, since 2024 Free inspection

Key takeaways

  • October is a good time to check the building before cold pushes rodents indoors.
  • Walk the outside at ground level and the roofline, looking for gaps about the size of a dime for mice and a quarter for rats, according to Texas A&M guidelines.
  • Move trash cans at least 30 feet from the building and store pet food in sealed containers.
  • Fix leaks and standing water, since rats need about an ounce of water a day.
  • If you find droppings, wet clean them with gloves. Never sweep or vacuum.

Why a checklist helps

Most rodent problems start with a gap, a food source or a water source. A short, regular check finds all three. This list is meant to be done in a weekend and repeated each fall.

We call it an October checklist because that is when many people think about winterizing. The steps are the same any time of year.

Outside the building

Walk the foundation and look for gaps, cracks and holes. Texas A&M says mice can use a quarter inch crack and rats a half inch.

Check where pipes, gas lines, dryer vents and cables enter the wall. Look at the corners of the garage door and the weather seal along the bottom. Cover openings with hardware cloth or rust-proof fill fabric, which Texas A&M names.

The roofline

Look from the ground at vents, soffits, flashing and places where pipes pass through the roof. Branches that touch the roof are a path for roof rats and should be trimmed back.

Binoculars help. Do not climb a ladder alone.

Food, trash and water

Move trash cans at least 30 feet from the building, as Texas A&M advises, and keep lids tight. Store pet food and bird seed in sealed containers and bring pet bowls in at night.

Fix leaking hose bibs and drips under sinks. Do not leave standing water against the foundation.

Garage, shed and storage

Lift boxes off the floor and leave a gap along the wall so you can see activity. Cardboard, fabric and paper are nesting material.

Stack firewood off the ground and away from walls.

Inside the house

Check under sinks, behind appliances, in cabinet bases and in the attic. Look for droppings, gnaw marks and nests, the signs Texas A&M lists.

If you find droppings, air the room out first. The CDC says to ventilate for 30 minutes, wet the droppings with a solution of 1.5 cups of bleach per gallon of water, wait five minutes and wipe. Do not sweep or vacuum.

If you find something

Write down what you found and where, and take photos. If it is more than a few droppings or you hear animals, call a professional. We inspect for free, can offer same-day service when you call early and answer 24/7. We are locally owned and started in 2024.

A printable weekend plan

Saturday morning, walk the outside: foundation, doors, vents and roofline. Photograph every gap you find. Saturday afternoon, buy hardware cloth, a door sweep and sealed bins and make the simple repairs.

Sunday morning, check the garage, shed and attic. Lift stored boxes off the floor. Sunday afternoon, move trash cans, clean up the yard and check hose bibs and drip lines for leaks.

What to do about gaps you cannot reach

Some gaps are high on a wall or on a steep roof. Do not climb to reach them. Note where they are and call a crew. We can inspect and seal the roofline for you, and we offer a free inspection.

Do not stuff paper or foam into a gap and call it done. Rodents chew soft materials, and hardware cloth or metal flashing lasts.

Signs that it is already too late for prevention

Fresh droppings, a smell of urine, gnawing, scratching in the walls or sightings in daylight mean animals are inside. At that point, prevention turns into removal.

Trap first, using the pre-baiting advice from Texas A&M, and seal afterward. If you are not sure how to start, call.

Repeating the checklist

The best habit is to do this walk every year. Write the date on your calendar, keep the photos, and compare. New gaps show up as buildings settle and weather wears seals.

Tools and materials to have on hand

Gather these before you start: a flashlight, gloves, a mask, a tape measure, hardware cloth, tin snips, rust-proof fill fabric or metal flashing, a door sweep, sealant that cures hard and a few sealed storage bins. Texas A&M names hardware cloth and rust-proof fill fabric for sealing, so those are the main materials to buy.

Hardware store staff can show you the right gauge of mesh. Cut pieces larger than the gap and fasten them to solid material with screws or staples rather than pushing them into the hole.

Common mistakes in fall prep

Many people seal the gaps they can see from inside and forget the outside. Others put out bait without knowing where mice travel and attract more animals. A third mistake is stacking firewood right against the wall, which gives rodents cover and a short route to the building.

Do the outside first, keep wood away from the wall and ask a professional before using bait.

A note on safety

Do not work on a roof alone, and wear gloves and a mask when you reach into attics, crawl spaces or sheds that have been closed. The CDC says to ventilate closed spaces for 30 minutes before cleaning.

If you find a nest, droppings in large amounts or a dead animal, stop and call. It is better to have a crew handle it than to risk exposure.

Checklist summary you can copy

Outside: foundation gaps, door seals, vents, utility entries, roofline, branches. Food and water: trash cans 30 feet away, pet food sealed, leaks fixed. Storage: boxes off the floor, firewood off the wall. Inside: under sinks, behind appliances, attic and garage. Cleanup: ventilate, wet clean, no sweeping or vacuuming.

If a step turns up a problem you cannot fix yourself, write it down and call. We inspect for free, offer same-day service when you call early, and answer 24/7.

Frequently asked questions.

When should I do this checklist?
Early fall is a good time, but any time you have not checked in a year is fine.
What is the cheapest step with the most effect?
Moving trash cans away from the building and sealing obvious gaps.
Should I set traps now?
Only if you see signs. Texas A&M advises pre-baiting traps for one to two weeks before setting them.
