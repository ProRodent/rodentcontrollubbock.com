Rodent control vs. general pest control in Lubbock, why specialists get better results.

By Lubbock Rodent Control · Published November 17, 2026 · 6 min read
Free inspection Written quote before work Locally Owned Open 24/7

Key takeaways

  • General pest control and rodent-specific control are different services, and the difference goes beyond price. The inspection scope, species identification process, exclusion approach, and follow-up structure are all different.
  • What most general pest companies skip: Species ID before treatment, weep-hole sealing as standard practice, follow-up confirmation that activity has stopped.
  • General pest control is appropriate for: Concurrent insect programs, light occasional rodent activity with no established interior population, properties that need quarterly pest management across multiple pest categories.
  • Rodent specialist is appropriate for: Confirmed interior population, roof-rat or Norway rat infestation requiring species-specific treatment, any situation requiring permanent exclusion sealing.
  • The two aren't mutually exclusive: Some Lubbock homeowners run a general pest quarterly program and call a rodent specialist when rodent activity appears. This is a reasonable approach.

General pest control and rodent-specific control are not the same service. They're often priced similarly, marketed to the same homeowners, and provided by different types of companies: but the scope, methodology, and outcomes differ. This guide explains what each service actually includes, where the gaps in general pest programs typically appear for rodent problems, and how to decide which type of service your situation requires.

What a general pest control program typically includes for rodent activity.

A standard quarterly general pest control program: the kind marketed as full protection against insects, spiders, and rodents, typically addresses rodents through exterior bait station placement. The technician places tamper-resistant bait stations around the foundation perimeter, services them quarterly, and monitors for consumption. This is a population-reduction tool, not a population-elimination tool. It reduces the rodent colony that reaches the structure's exterior, but it doesn't address the entry points through which rodents are already inside, and it doesn't identify the species involved or tailor the treatment approach to that species.

A general pest technician on a quarterly route visit, typically 20 to 45 minutes for an average residential property, doesn't have time to conduct a full species-ID inspection, map interior runways, assess weep-hole condition across the full foundation perimeter, or perform exclusion work. Those services aren't part of what the quarterly rate covers, and a technician who is scheduled for 15 stops in a day can't reasonably deliver them on a standard service visit.

What a rodent specialist does differently.

Species ID before treatment. Roof rats, Norway rats, and house mice require completely different treatment approaches. Roof-rat treatment requires above-grade bait stations positioned on attic runways, roof-line exclusion, and attic inspection. Norway rat treatment requires ground-level burrow treatment, exterior bait stations positioned on Norway rat runways (not the same locations as roof-rat stations), and foundation sealing. House mouse treatment requires interior snap-trap deployment on confirmed wall-base runways and weep-hole sealing. Applying the wrong program to the wrong species produces poor results regardless of product quality. A rodent specialist identifies the species from physical evidence, droppings, runway location, nesting height, before placing anything.

Exclusion as a standard part of the program. Permanent exclusion, sealing the entry points that allowed the colony to establish, is what prevents recurrence. Most general pest quarterly programs don't include exclusion work as a standard deliverable; it's either not offered or offered as an add-on at significant additional cost. A rodent specialist includes entry-point assessment in the initial inspection and exclusion work as part of the program's scope, because treatment without exclusion produces the same problem the following season.

Follow-up confirmation. A rodent specialist should return 10 to 14 days after treatment to confirm that activity has stopped: checking traps, assessing new dropping activity, and verifying that the exclusion work has held. This follow-up is what distinguishes a complete program from a single treatment visit. Without the follow-up, neither the company nor the homeowner knows whether the program worked.

Where general pest programs perform well: and where they don't.

General pest programs perform well for properties that need ongoing insect management across multiple pest categories, ants, spiders, cockroaches, wasps, with light concurrent rodent monitoring. The quarterly exterior bait station program provides a meaningful deterrent against rodent pressure building up at the foundation perimeter. For properties without an established interior infestation and with intact exclusion barriers, quarterly general pest service is appropriate and cost-effective.

General pest programs reliably underperform for established interior infestations. Once house mice are in the wall voids or roof rats are in the attic, exterior bait station replenishment doesn't address the interior population. The rodents with interior territories have access to interior food sources, nesting material, and shelter, so the exterior bait station competes poorly with all of those attractants for an animal that's already established inside. The correct approach for an established interior infestation is interior population reduction (trapping or interior bait stations on confirmed interior runways) followed by exclusion, neither of which is in a standard general pest quarterly program.

The decision framework for Lubbock homeowners.

Use a general pest program if: you want ongoing quarterly management of insects and other pests with concurrent rodent monitoring, you have no current evidence of an established interior rodent colony, and your home has relatively intact exclusion barriers (functional door sweeps, sealed weep holes, intact soffit vents). Add a rodent specialist call if: you have confirmed interior evidence (droppings in more than one room, audible activity in walls or attic, live or dead rodents found inside), the species involved is a rat (roof or Norway), or you want permanent exclusion work performed on your foundation perimeter and roof line.

The two approaches aren't mutually exclusive. Some Lubbock homeowners maintain a quarterly general pest program for insect management and call us when rodent activity appears. This is a reasonable and cost-effective approach. What doesn't work is treating an established rodent infestation with a general pest exterior bait station program and expecting it to resolve, that's a population deterrent being asked to do the work of population elimination and exclusion.

Frequently asked questions.

Is Lubbock Rodent Control a general pest company or a rodent specialist?
Rodent specialist. We don't treat insects, spiders, or other pest categories: we focus exclusively on rodent ID, treatment, exclusion, and cleanup. This focus is why the inspection process, species ID, and exclusion work are deeper than what a general pest quarterly program delivers.
Can I keep my general pest quarterly program and also use a rodent specialist?
Yes: and this is a reasonable approach for many Lubbock homeowners. The quarterly general pest program handles insect management and provides exterior rodent monitoring. When rodent activity appears, call us for the species-specific inspection and treatment. The two programs don't conflict.
What's the sign that my general pest program isn't handling the rodent problem?
Ongoing interior evidence despite active quarterly service: droppings in kitchens or living spaces, audible scratching in walls or attic, rodents seen inside the structure. Exterior bait station consumption decreasing while interior evidence continues means the exterior program is reducing the exterior population but not addressing the established interior colony.
Do general pest companies do exclusion work?
Some do, typically as a significant add-on to the standard quarterly program. The quality varies considerably. Ask specifically what materials are used for weep-hole sealing (the correct answer is copper mesh or hardware cloth, not foam), what warranty is provided on exclusion work, and whether a follow-up inspection is included to verify the work held.
Is a rodent specialist more expensive than a general pest company?
For a single treatment visit, comparable or sometimes higher. For a complete program including exclusion, the rodent specialist is typically more expensive because the scope is broader. The comparison that matters is outcome: a general pest exterior program that doesn't resolve an interior infestation costs whatever it costs plus the ongoing damage from an unaddressed colony.
How do I know if I have an established interior infestation vs. occasional rodent pressure?
Established interior infestation signs: droppings in more than one room or multiple locations in one room, droppings in protected spaces (cabinet bases, behind appliances) where rodents feel safe traveling regularly, audible scratching in walls or attic on multiple nights, visible gnaw marks on food packaging or structural materials. Occasional pressure signs: single droppings near a door or garage entry, one rodent seen and not seen again, droppings only at the perimeter.

What Texas licensing says about structural pest control

In Texas, anyone in the business of structural pest control needs a business license from the Texas Department of Agriculture for each location, and the definition covers sales, pesticide applications, pest control devices, and inspection work. The license requires a designated certified applicator and proof of general liability insurance.

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension describes rodent work as a mix of exclusion, trapping, and sanitation, with trapping needing almost daily maintenance. That mix of methods is what separates a rodent program from a routine perimeter spray.
