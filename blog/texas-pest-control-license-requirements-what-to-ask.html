Texas pest control license requirements: what to ask before hiring a Lubbock exterminator.

By Lubbock Rodent Control · Published November 17, 2026 · 6 min read
Free inspection Written quote before work Locally Owned Open 24/7

Key takeaways

  • Texas licenses structural pest control businesses: A company that sells or performs pest control work on structures, rodent work included, needs a business license from the Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA) for each location, and a certified applicator has to be designated to run it.
  • Check the license before you sign: TDA publishes the business license information. Ask for the company's license number and confirm it with TDA.
  • Insurance is a separate question you can ask: TDA rules require licensed businesses to maintain general liability insurance with set minimums. Ask for a certificate of insurance anyway.
  • Technicians matter too: Employees must be registered with the licensed business, and new hires without a license start as registered apprentices. Ask who will be on your roof or in your attic.
  • Get the scope in writing: The contract should list the entry points, the materials, the follow-up visits, and the terms for re-treatment.

Hiring a rodent control company in Lubbock should be simple. In practice, the Texas licensing rules for structural pest control are not widely known, and unlicensed operators advertise "rodent proofing" alongside the licensed ones. This guide covers what the state requires, how to check a company, and what to ask before you sign a contract.

What Texas requires of a pest control business.

Structural pest control in Texas is regulated by the Texas Department of Agriculture. Under TDA's rules, anyone in the business of structural pest control needs a business license for each location, including branch offices. The rules define "business" broadly: it covers sales and service, pesticide applications, the use of pest control devices, and inspection or identification work. A company may not advertise or solicit pest control work until its license is issued.

Each commercial licensee must also designate a responsible certified applicator who runs the license category. That person cannot hold the role for another business or location. Employees have to be registered with the licensed business within 10 days of hire, and workers without a technician or certified applicator license begin as registered apprentices. The training requirements for technicians and certified applicators are set out in the Texas Administrative Code, Title 4, sections 7.133 and 7.134.

How to check a license before hiring.

Ask the company for its business license number, then confirm it with the Texas Department of Agriculture, which administers structural pest control licensing. A license lapses if the renewal fee and proof of insurance are not received by the expiration date, so a license that looks fine on a truck door can still be expired. TDA also requires licensed businesses to print a TDA jurisdiction statement on contracts and similar documents, so look for it on the paperwork you are handed.

The check takes a few minutes. If a company declines to give you a license number when you ask, treat that as a reason to keep looking.

Why unlicensed exclusion work is a risk in Lubbock.

Sealing weep holes, screening soffit vents, and blocking utility penetrations looks like ordinary home maintenance from the outside. That makes it easy for handymen and general contractors to offer "exclusion" work. Whether a particular job counts as structural pest control under state rules is a question for TDA, and a homeowner is better off asking the regulator than guessing.

The practical risk is work quality. Exclusion done well depends on species-specific details: copper mesh rather than foam in weep holes, because foam breaks down and does not stop gnawing; 1/4-inch hardware cloth as a common screening size, because larger mesh passes house mice; and a seal that still lets the weep hole drain. Exclusion done without that knowledge often fails within a season or two.

Three questions to ask before hiring.

Question 1: What is your structural pest control business license number? This is the baseline. Write it down and confirm it with TDA before signing.

Question 2: Who will do the work, and how are they registered or licensed? A company license does not automatically make every worker a licensed technician. Ask for the name of the person assigned to your job and how that person is registered with the business.

Question 3: Can I see a certificate of liability insurance? TDA rules require licensed businesses to carry general liability insurance covering bodily injury and property damage, with minimum limits set in the rules, and the policy must delete the care, custody, and control exclusion. A certificate is easy to produce for a company that carries one.

What to watch for in the contract.

A rodent control contract should state the treatment methods, the specific entry points to be sealed and the materials to be used, the follow-up visit schedule, and the terms for re-treatment if activity continues. A good operator scopes the whole job at the inspection and quotes the complete program. Be cautious with bids that grow after work begins because "more entry points were found."

What the state's own pages say.

TDA's structural pest control pages state that a business license is required for each location, that a certified applicator must be designated, and that the license requires current proof of insurance. TDA's rules also set minimum general liability coverage for bodily injury and property damage, and they require that the care, custody, and control exclusion be deleted from the policy. Frequently asked questions.

How do I check a Texas pest control license?
Ask the company for its business license number and confirm it with the Texas Department of Agriculture, which administers structural pest control licensing. A current license has not passed its expiration date, and TDA requires licensed businesses to print a jurisdiction statement on contracts.
What should a licensed pest control company have besides the license?
Texas rules require licensed structural pest control businesses to maintain general liability insurance for bodily injury and property damage, with minimum limits set by TDA. Ask for a certificate of insurance, and ask who the designated certified applicator is.
Does a pest control license cover every worker on the crew?
No. The business holds the license and designates a responsible certified applicator. Employees must be registered with the business within 10 days of hire, and workers without a technician or certified applicator license start as registered apprentices. Ask who will be doing your job.
Can a general contractor do rodent exclusion work without a pest control license?
Texas defines structural pest control work broadly, and the question of whether a specific job falls under it is one for the Texas Department of Agriculture. Ask any contractor offering rodent exclusion for a TDA business license number before you hire.
What if I hire an unlicensed operator and something goes wrong?
You may have limited recourse. A business operating outside the licensing system may not carry the insurance that licensed businesses must maintain, so a claim for failed work or property damage could be against an individual with little coverage. A licensed company's insurance exists to cover those situations.
What does it mean if a company has a disciplinary action on its TDA record?
It means TDA took action against the license. Review what the action was and how it was resolved before hiring. One administrative lapse that was promptly fixed is different from a pattern of consumer complaints.
