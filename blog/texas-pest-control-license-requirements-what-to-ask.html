Texas pest control license requirements: what to ask before hiring a Lubbock exterminator.

Written by Lubbock Rodent Control. Updated November 2026.
By Lubbock Rodent Control · Published November 17, 2026 · 6 min read
Open 24/7 Same-day service Locally owned, since 2024 Free inspection

Key takeaways

  • Texas requires structural pest control businesses to be licensed, and applicators to hold a certified applicator license.
  • The Texas Department of Agriculture handles these licenses.
  • You can contact TDA at (800) TELL-TDA to confirm a license.
  • Ask about method, not only paperwork: how entries will be found and sealed.
  • Get the plan in writing before work starts.

What the state says

The Texas Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control Service says businesses that perform structural pest control for hire must be licensed. Individuals who apply pesticides or perform inspections for hire need a certified applicator license, which requires passing an exam. Technicians and apprentices are registered separately.

Applicator categories include pest control, termite control, lawn and ornamental, structural fumigation, commodity fumigation, weed control and wood preservation.

How to check

The TDA page does not describe a public online license lookup. It lists the Texas Agriculture Portal for applications and renewals and a phone line, (800) TELL-TDA, for questions. To confirm a specific license, contact TDA directly.

Ask the company for its license number and the name of the person who will do the work, then confirm with the state.

Ask about the method

A license shows that a company is registered. It does not tell you how they will solve your problem. Ask how entry points will be found, what materials will seal them and how traps or bait will be placed.

Texas A&M describes hardware cloth and rust-proof fill fabric for sealing and pre-baiting of traps for one to two weeks. A provider who knows this is likely to do careful work.

Ask about cleanup

Droppings and nests need safe cleanup. The CDC says to ventilate, wear gloves, wet the droppings with a bleach solution and not sweep or vacuum. Ask who will do this and how.

Ask about follow up

Rodent work can take more than one visit. Ask what is included in repeat visits and what happens if activity continues. Get it in writing.

A short script for the phone call

You can use these questions with any company. Is your business licensed with the Texas Department of Agriculture, and can I have the number? Who will do the work, and are they a certified applicator or a registered technician? How will you find the entry points? What will you use to seal them? Who cleans up droppings and nests?

Then ask about follow up. How many visits are expected, what happens if activity continues, and will I get the plan in writing?

Red flags

Be careful of anyone who will not give a license number, who quotes a price over the phone without seeing the building, or who only offers spraying for a rat or mouse problem. Rodent control depends on sealing and trapping.

Also be careful if someone asks for full payment up front or pushes you to sign the same minute. A plan that holds up can wait a day.

What happens if something goes wrong

The TDA website lists a Report Fraud page for concerns about unlicensed activity. Keep your written plan, photos and notes. If the work does not hold, call the company first and give them a chance to fix it.

If you still have an issue, the state is a place to take it.

Why method matters as much as paperwork

Two licensed companies can do very different work. One may spray around the foundation and leave. Another may find entries, seal them with hardware cloth, trap with patience and clean up with the CDC steps. The license is the starting point, not the whole answer.

Ask each company to walk you through a typical job from first visit to last, then compare.

Keeping your own records

Keep a folder with the written plan, photos before and after sealing, receipts and the dates of each visit. If activity returns, those records tell you what was done and where gaps might remain.

Write down the name of the person who did the work and the license number they gave you.

What to ask about chemicals and bait

If bait is part of the plan, ask where it will go, how it is secured and how pets and children are protected. Bait should be in tamper-resistant stations and out of reach. Ask for a map of station locations.

Ask what happens to any animal that dies in a wall, since that can cause odor. A good provider has a plan for it.

Quick checklist before you hire

License number given and confirmed with TDA. Name of the person doing the work. Written plan with entry points, materials and number of visits. Cleanup included or priced separately. Follow up described. Bait stations mapped and tamper resistant.

If a company will not answer any of these, try another. A good provider expects the questions.

Keeping the conversation simple

You do not need to be an expert to hire well. Three things matter most: a confirmed license, a clear plan for finding and sealing entries and a clear plan for cleanup. If you get those, the rest is details.

Take notes during the call, ask the company to send the plan in writing and give yourself a day to compare.

Questions to ask about method

Whatever a provider's paperwork shows, you can compare the method with published guidelines. Texas A&M describes sealing gaps with hardware cloth, pre-baiting traps for one to two weeks and checking them almost daily. The CDC describes wet cleaning of droppings. Ask whether the plan includes these steps.

Source for "Questions to ask about method" (published guidelines): Texas Department of Agriculture, Structural Pest Control Licensing; Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM; CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents.

Frequently asked questions.

Who licenses pest control in Texas?
The Texas Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control Service.
Do technicians need a license?
Technicians and apprentices are registered or licensed separately, according to TDA.
How do I verify a license?
Contact TDA at (800) TELL-TDA, since the page does not describe a public lookup.
Is a license enough?
No. Ask about method, cleanup and follow up too.
