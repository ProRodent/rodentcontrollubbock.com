The gaps people miss

A quarter inch crack under a door or a dime sized hole is enough for a mouse, according to Texas A&M. Common ones are around gas lines, dryer vents, garage door corners and cable entries.

We check the outside of the building at ground level and the roofline.

Materials that work

Texas A&M names hardware cloth and rust-proof fill fabric for sealing. We use metal mesh, sealant for the edges and door sweeps where doors leave gaps.

Foam alone can be chewed through, so we do not rely on it.

Timing the work

Seal after the mice are out. If animals are still inside a closed wall, they can die there or find another way in.

We time the work with trapping so each step supports the other.

What a sealing visit looks like

We go around the outside of the building at ground level, then inside along walls and under sinks. Each gap is marked, photographed and sealed.

You get a summary of what we closed and what to watch.

Mouse sized gaps

A mouse can enter through a hole about the size of a dime or a quarter inch crack, according to Texas A&M. That includes gaps around pipes, under doors and at utility lines.

Seal with hardware cloth or rust-proof fill fabric, the materials the guide names.

Source for "Mouse sized gaps" (published guidelines): Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM.
