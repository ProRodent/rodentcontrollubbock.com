What rodents do under a house

A crawl space is dark, quiet and often damp, and pipes and insulation give nesting material. Rodents can travel from there up through gaps around plumbing into wall cavities.

Damaged insulation and droppings under the floor can also lead to smells indoors.

How we seal a crawl space

We check the vents, the access door, the sill and every place pipes or wires pass through. Openings are closed with hardware cloth or similar metal mesh, fastened to masonry or wood.

We keep airflow where the building needs it, because trapped moisture causes its own damage.

Moisture and drainage

Water pooled near the foundation helps rodents and weakens the structure. Downspouts that dump against the wall and soil that slopes toward the house are worth fixing.

We tell you what we see, even when it is outside our service.

Getting ready for a crawl space visit

Make sure the access door is clear and tell us about any standing water or past plumbing work. We bring lighting and protective gear.

We photograph the vents and penetrations we seal so you have a record, and flag any moisture or drainage issues we notice.

Foundation vent and opening checks

Crawl space vents and access panels are common entries. Texas A&M names hardware cloth and rust-proof fill fabric as sealing materials.

Seal the gap, not only the hole you see. A rat can use about a half inch crack.

Source for "Foundation vent and opening checks" (published guidelines): Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM.
