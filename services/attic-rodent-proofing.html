Attic rodent proofing in Lubbock, TX.

Where rodents get into an attic

Roof vents, gable ends, the edge where the roof meets the wall and the places where pipes and wires pass through are the usual entries. Tree branches and wires touching the roof give animals a path up.

From the ground, the damage often looks small. Up close, a gap the size of a quarter is enough for a rat.

How we proof an attic

We inspect from inside and outside, mark each opening and photograph it. Gaps are closed with hardware cloth, metal flashing or other rodent-proof material, fastened so it cannot be pushed aside.

Vents stay open for airflow, with mesh over them. We do not seal anything until we are sure the animals are out.

What comes after proofing

Once entries are closed, we recommend a short monitoring period. Fresh droppings or noise after sealing mean a gap was missed or an animal is still inside.

If there is contamination, cleanup and insulation work can follow.

A typical proofing visit

We start outside with the roofline and vents, then go into the attic to match what we saw from the ground. Each gap is photographed so you can see what was sealed.

If animals are still inside, trapping comes first and sealing follows once activity stops.

Where attic gaps appear

Attic entries are often at the roofline, soffits, vents and where pipes pass through. According to Texas A&M, rats need only about a quarter sized hole or a half inch crack.

Cover openings with hardware cloth fastened firmly. Foam alone can be chewed through.

Source for "Where attic gaps appear" (published guidelines): Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM.

Questions about attic rodent proofing

Will proofing keep rodents out for good?
Sealing removes the entries we can find, and monitoring catches anything missed. Trimming branches and storing food properly helps too.
Do you seal before or after trapping?
After. Sealing with animals still inside can trap them in the walls.
