Commercial rodent pressure in Lubbock.

Audit-grade documentation. Per visit.

Commercial properties face rodent pressure from sources that residential properties do not. Restaurant dumpster areas, loading dock perimeters, and outdoor grease traps create food-source concentrations that attract Norway rats year-round. The Loop 289 industrial districts and warehouse corridors see ground-level pressure from both Norway rats and house mice drawn to stored goods and the structural gaps common in metal-construction buildings. Medical facilities near Texas Tech have stricter documentation requirements for any rodent activity or treatment.

The treatment approach for a commercial account also differs from residential work. The program needs to run without disrupting operations, and it needs to produce service records that satisfy health department inspectors or property management audits. Every commercial program we run includes written service reports after each visit.

What commercial rodent control includes.

The full sequence. Nothing skipped.

  • Property assessment and program design: review of the property layout, food and waste storage, and current pest activity before proposing any program.
  • Exterior bait station network: tamper-resistant commercial-grade stations on the property perimeter, serviced on a regular rotation schedule.
  • Interior trap program: snap or glue traps in back-of-house areas, storage rooms, and mechanical spaces where rodent activity is found.
  • Entry-point exclusion: dock door seals, pipe penetration sealing, and foundation gap closures using materials rated for commercial use.
  • Service documentation: written report after every visit including station status, activity findings, and exclusion work completed.
  • Scheduled rotation: monthly or bi-weekly service visits depending on property type and pressure level.

Frequently asked questions.

Do you provide documentation for health department inspections?
Yes. Every service visit generates a written report that includes the date, technician, station activity findings, trap results, and any exclusion work completed. These records are maintained and available for health department or property management review.
Can you service my restaurant during off-hours to avoid disruption?
Yes. We schedule commercial service visits around your operating hours. For restaurants, we typically schedule early morning visits before kitchen prep begins, or late evening after close.
How many bait stations does my property need?
Station count is determined by the property perimeter length, the number of entry points, and the level of existing pressure. A typical small commercial property runs 6-12 stations; larger properties and warehouses may need 20 or more. We propose a specific station map during the initial assessment.
What happens if we find rodent activity between scheduled service visits?
Call us at (806) 207-3665 and we will dispatch a service visit outside the regular schedule. Unscheduled visits for active activity situations are part of every commercial program.
Do you serve medical facilities near Texas Tech?
Yes. We are familiar with the documentation and treatment-timing requirements for medical facilities and coordinate with facility managers to meet access and protocol requirements.
What is the difference between a commercial program and buying bait stations at a hardware store?
Commercial-grade tamper-resistant stations are rated for public access areas, consumer stations are not. Commercial programs include station mapping, activity tracking, written documentation, and scheduled service with a trained technician.
Do you serve commercial properties outside Lubbock?
Yes: Wolfforth, Slaton, Levelland, and other South Plains towns within roughly 45 minutes. Multi-location accounts in the region are quoted on a portfolio basis.
