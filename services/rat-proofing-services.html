Rat proofing services in Lubbock, TX: roof-line, soffit, and attic exclusion.

Last reviewed: January 2026

Rat proofing in Lubbock covers both species at both levels. Roof rats need the above-grade work (soffits, fascia, gable vents, roof-line gaps). Norway rats need the ground-level work (weep holes, foundation cracks, garage door sweeps, utility line entries). A real rat-proofing job inspects both elevations of the building because Lubbock has both species, and a property that proofs only one level still loses to the other.

Rat proofing is the structural exclusion process that seals the specific entry points used by roof rats and Norway rats: larger gaps located at roof-line vents, fascia junctions, foundation penetrations, and burrow-access points. In Lubbock, TX, rat proofing after treatment is the step that breaks the annual reactivation cycle that many homeowners have come to accept as inevitable.

Roof-line and attic rat proofing vs. general exclusion.

Documented at every step.

Rat proofing focuses on the larger gaps, 1/2 inch or larger, that roof rats and Norway rats need to access a structure. This is distinct from mouse-grade exclusion, which addresses 1/4-inch gaps. The locations are also different: rats primarily enter at roof-line elevation (soffit vents, gable vents, the fascia-sheathing junction) or at foundation level near burrow-access points. The materials need to stop gnawing: hardware cloth, sheet metal, and concrete patch.

What rat proofing includes.

The full sequence. Nothing skipped.

  • Roof-line inspection and sealing: soffit vents covered with hardware cloth; gable vents reinforced; fascia-sheathing gaps blocked with sheet metal or wood blocking and mesh.
  • Foundation and burrow access sealing: Norway rat entry at pipe penetrations, drain access points, and foundation cracks sealed with concrete patch and hardware cloth.
  • Tree and vegetation assessment: overhanging branches that provide roof-rat roof access flagged for trimming.
  • Utility chase sealing: all pipe and conduit penetrations through the exterior wall sealed with foam-and-mesh appropriate for the gap size.
  • Before-and-after documentation: photo record of every sealed point provided at job completion.

Frequently asked questions.

What is the most important rat proofing step in Lubbock?
For roof rats: soffit vent coverage with hardware cloth and fascia-gap blocking. These are the two locations roof rats use most reliably to enter Lubbock attics. For Norway rats, pipe penetration sealing at the foundation and drain-cover gap closing.
Does rat proofing also stop mice?
Partially. Rat-grade sealing at 1/2-inch gaps stops rats but not mice, which can compress through 1/4-inch openings. For complete exclusion, mouse-grade sealing at weep holes and smaller penetrations needs to be added. We typically do both levels together.
How long do the sealing materials last?
Hardware cloth and sheet metal properly fastened will last 10-plus years under Lubbock conditions. Foam-and-mesh at utility penetrations may need recheck at 3-5 years in high-movement areas. We note expected material lifespan on the documentation provided at completion.
Can you rat-proof my home before I have had an infestation?
Yes: preventive rat proofing is a valid service. If your neighbors have had roof-rat problems or your home has mature trees adjacent to the roof line, preventive sealing in August or September before cold-front season is a sound investment.
Do you trim trees as part of rat proofing?
We flag overhanging branches and provide trimming recommendations. We do not provide tree trimming ourselves, but we can identify which branches are primary access routes versus incidental.
What if rats chew through the exclusion materials?
Hardware cloth and sheet metal cannot be chewed through by rats. Foam alone can. We never rely on foam as the sole exclusion material at any location a rat can access.
Is rat proofing worth it if I have never had a problem?
If you have a Tech Terrace, Overton, or Heart of Lubbock address with mature trees and an older roof line, yes, the prevention cost is significantly lower than remediation after an established colony.
