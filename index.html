How a job works

Inspect, seal, trap, clean up.

  1. Inspect. We look for the signs the Texas A&M guide lists: sightings, noises, gnaw marks, nests, burrows, droppings, urine stains, rub marks and pet alerts. Then we trace each one to an entry point.
  2. Seal. Mice use a gap the size of a dime and rats one the size of a quarter. Gaps are closed with hardware cloth or rust-proof fill fabric, not foam alone.
  3. Trap. Rats avoid new objects, so traps can be left unset with food for one to two weeks first. Mice are trapped along walls within about 20 to 50 feet of the nest.
  4. Clean up. We follow the CDC wet method: ventilate, wear gloves, spray with disinfectant, wait five minutes, wipe up and bag it. Droppings are never swept or vacuumed.

Source for this section: Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM and CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents.

Why local matters

What Lubbock's climate means for rodents.

Lubbock sits at 3,202 feet on the Llano Estacado and averages 18.33 inches of rain and 7 inches of snow a year. Winter mornings usually fall below freezing, and lows reach 10 degrees or lower on about 1.5 mornings a year. House mice live outdoors in summer according to Texas A&M, so the first cold nights are when calls about noises in attics, garages and kitchens start.

Rats need about one ounce of water a day, and many Lubbock parks hold small playa lakes used as stormwater reservoirs. Water near a foundation, whether from a park, a drip or a downspout, helps a burrow last through the dry months.

Source for this section: Wikipedia, Lubbock, Texas and Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM.

Common questions

Before you call.

What should I do first if I find droppings?
Do not sweep or vacuum them. The CDC says to ventilate the area for 30 minutes, wear rubber or plastic gloves, spray with disinfectant or a bleach solution of 1.5 cups per gallon of water, let it soak for 5 minutes, then wipe up with paper towels and bag it.
Is hantavirus a risk in Texas?
The Texas Department of State Health Services says the first human case in Texas was reported in 1993, that people can be infected through contact with the urine, droppings or saliva of infected rodents, and that there is no treatment for the disease in people. Sealing openings and removing food, water and shelter are its prevention advice.
Why seal gaps before trapping?
Trapping removes the animals that are inside, but open gaps let more in. The Texas A&M guide says to close any hole, crack or gap, and that a mouse can use a dime sized hole while a rat needs a quarter sized one.
Are poison baits a good first step?
They carry risk. The Texas A&M guide says second generation anticoagulants are highly toxic, persist in body tissue and can harm pets, wildlife and people. For a suspected animal poisoning it directs readers to the National Pesticide Information Center at 1-800-858-7378.

Sources: CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents; Texas DSHS, Hantavirus FAQ; Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM.

Practical first steps before you call

Most rodent problems start with an unsealed gap, available food or a water source. According to Texas A&M, a mouse can use a quarter inch crack and a rat about a half inch, so a quick walk around the building is worth the time.

Move trash cans away from the wall, store pet food in sealed containers, fix leaks and photograph anything you find. Do not sweep or vacuum droppings, according to the CDC.

Source for "Practical first steps before you call" (published guidelines): Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM; CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents.
